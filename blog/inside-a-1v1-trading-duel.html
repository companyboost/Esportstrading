Inside a 1v1 Trading Duel. 60 minutes, multiple charts, one winner

Explainer 26 September 2026 5 min read

The 1v1 duel is the atom of Esports Trading. Every league, tournament and team format is built on top of it, and it is the format most people watch first. Here is how one works from the opening bell to the final ranking update.

The setup

Two traders. Multiple markets, usually liquid pairs such as BTC/USD and ETH/USD. One fixed window, typically 60 minutes. Both competitors start with an identical balance and identical rules on leverage, position size and the number of open trades. Nothing about the setup favours either side. The only variable is the trader.

The rules

  • Both traders see the same live charts and the same order books at the same time.
  • Every order, fill and balance is recorded and verified through the platform, so results are provable rather than claimed.
  • The clock is the clock. When the window closes, all open positions are marked to market and the duel ends.
  • The higher net return wins. A tie goes to the trader with the lower drawdown.

What spectators see

This is where the duel stops being a spreadsheet and becomes a sport. Viewers watch both traders' returns update live on a single overlay, see every entry and exit as it happens, and hear commentators break down the decisions in real time. A trader who is up early and sits still is playing defence. A trader who is down and starts sizing up is going for the comeback. The story writes itself.

Beating the market is a number. Beating another trader, live, in front of a crowd, is a moment.

Why it matters for rankings

Every sanctioned duel feeds a trader's record: wins, losses, streaks, average return and average drawdown. Those records roll up into the ETPS score that the Esports Trading League is developing to rank competitors across platforms. Win enough duels and you move up the ladder toward tournament seeds and team contracts.

How to enter your first duel

Founding platforms such as FatCat Arena already run open 1v1 competitions. Create a competitor profile, pick a duel in the lobby, and you are in. New competitors are matched against similar records, so the first few duels are a fair test rather than a lesson.

Trade. Compete. Watch. Rank. The duel is where it starts.
